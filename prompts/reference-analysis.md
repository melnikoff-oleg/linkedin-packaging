# Deep visual reference analysis

Inspect the actual saved banner and portrait separately at source resolution, then together in a profile-layout preview. Read the exact saved headline. Do not produce a generic analysis based only on names or profile text.

For each banner, record:

- Source dimensions, aspect ratio, visible copy, and a source/capture reference.
- Every layer: background color/gradient/photo/texture, headline, supporting copy, CTA, brand identifier, logos, proof, portrait cutouts, decorative illustrations, lines, grids and shapes. Distinguish an ecosystem logo from a client/press endorsement.
- Bounding boxes in source pixels and normalized fractions, alignments, margins, text/graphic balance, baseline rhythm, spacing and overlap order. State that measurements are estimates from the raster.
- Typography: observable letterform traits, likely family/category, chosen reproduction substitute, weight, size, line-height, tracking, case, italic/underline/highlight and text wrapping. Never claim an exact font without evidence. Include confidence.
- Color samples, local contrast behind the actual text, hierarchy at full width and at normal display scale, and which small details can disappear without losing the message.

For each portrait, record head/face size, headroom, eye line, chin and shoulder crop; camera angle and posture; expression; background; clothing; lighting direction, softness and catchlights; color temperature; edge quality; texture/retouching; and likely circle-crop behavior. Describe visible features, not assumed personality or sensitive traits.

If the banner includes the same person, analyze that photograph independently from the avatar, then compare them in the assembled desktop and mobile profile. Record the distinct role of each image and differences in head tilt, gaze, expression, outfit, torso/hand visibility, body stance, environment and relative face size. A matching palette does not explain why a second face belongs in the banner. Do not assume a wider banner photograph is the avatar cropped or enlarged; equally, do not claim different source files from raster appearance alone.

For the combined system, assess avatar overlap, relative visual weight, palette continuity, repeated pose, competing focal points and mobile vulnerability at actual display scale. Ask what the banner photograph adds beyond recognition and whether its visible body frame survives the banner crop. A second close-up that repeats the avatar is a failure even when the two assets look polished separately. The lower-left quarter is a conservative protected region, not a fixed official UI measurement.

Use the corrected [portrait-role audit](../docs/portrait-role-audit.md) when adapting these references. The implemented Diandra, Lara, Matt, Rob and Jake directions require a distinct wider banner photograph; Clare, Adrian, Chris, Charlie and Liam contain no banner person. Charlie's dated multi-person event lineup has a participant-identification purpose that does not transfer to a repeated solo portrait. Do not invent an event, guests, follower counts or credentials to recreate that context.

For the headline, identify the role, buyer, outcome, mechanism, proof and CTA. Explain ordering, hooks, specificity, separators, rhythm and likely truncation. Record weaknesses as well as strengths. A large follower count does not prove the packaging caused that audience.

Save `analysis.md` and `design.json` per reference. Finish with a target-specific reconstruction recipe: what to borrow, what to simplify, which separate photo assets and poses are needed, what evidence fills each proof slot, and what must never be copied (identity, unverified brands/results, awards, testimonials, expired events). Review against the originals rather than judging the reconstruction alone. Keep this analysis available for construction and evidence checks, but exclude it from the independent first-impression review: that reviewer receives only the buyer persona and neutral assembled desktop/mobile profile cards.
