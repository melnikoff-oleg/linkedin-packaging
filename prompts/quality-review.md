# Final package quality review

Assess the package against the selected original reference and the target's source evidence, not only against its own previous version. Open the exported pixels; a successful render is not a visual approval.

Required deliverables: banner PNG, square portrait PNG/JPEG, desktop and mobile combined profile previews, and headline text. Supporting brief, editable HTML, separate photo-role provenance, neutral review cards and QA records remain local. The presentation previews must be labeled as simulations; they are not screenshots of a published LinkedIn profile. Neutral review cards omit generator labels and rationale so they do not prime the separate reviewer.

Run structural checks: 3168×792 banner (4:1), 800×800 portrait, correct image format, each LinkedIn upload below 8 MB, nonempty headline at most 220 characters, required files present, fonts loaded, no clipped/overflowing elements, no important content under the overlap mask. A check failure stops delivery until fixed.

Check photo roles before judging polish. Diandra, Lara, Matt, Rob and Jake require a distinct banner photograph and usable transparent cutout; Clare, Adrian, Chris, Charlie and Liam contain no banner person. Exact-hash and perceptual similarity checks reject identical or near-identical source images, including many resized, reencoded, monochrome and mirrored variants. A passing reuse check does not establish different poses or a credible whole-profile impression.

Then inspect visually:

1. Identity: compare the portrait with the actual source. Verify glasses, head shape, age, hairline, facial hair, eyes and skin detail. Reject identity drift even if the result is attractive.
2. Message and substitution: can a buyer unfamiliar with the brands understand the offer, business context and result in a few seconds, and learn something concrete about this particular person? Mentally remove the name and face. If almost any comparable professional could use the meaningful copy unchanged, reject it even when polished. Broad outcomes, job titles, an unexplained logo row, an own-company name and years of experience alone do not establish distinctive work. Require a relevant named company/project connected to a contribution, specific decision or scoped result; a memorable qualitative case can work without numbers. Keep unfamiliar names/logos as attribution, not the only explanation. Prefer industry/problem/result wording and metric-safe plain language. “Software releases” and “testing effort” must not become delivery speed or payroll savings. Do not invent evidence to satisfy a format.
3. Truth: trace every number, role, affiliation, award, logo, quote and implied endorsement to the target's evidence. Do not pass an invented claim because it sounds plausible.
4. Reference fidelity and design: open the original and output at the same width, side by side. Preserve original title size, type hierarchy, phrase lengths, line breaks, logo positions, card count/rhythm, portrait placement and negative space. Verify all source visual zones remain recognizable. The former universal 48 px support floor and single-proof-unit restriction are superseded for reference-faithful layouts. Do not enlarge all support labels, merge a collage into one card or add a proof footer to a sparse source. Inspect actual glyph ink, descenders, highlights, clipping and collisions; repair copy within the fixed slots. Check original transparent logos for correct proportions and no extra white plates. Truthful substitutions may change employer/press labels and unsupported proof slots may stay empty; document these explicitly. Coordinates and tests do not substitute for the direct aesthetic comparison.
5. Cohesion and photo roles: examine the actual banner/avatar/headline combination. Compare pose, head tilt, gaze, expression, body framing, clothing and face scale at normal display size. A wider banner shot must add stance or context; reject the same close-up pasted twice, even if the files differ and the individual images are attractive. Keep the avatar as the primary identity anchor, preserve enough banner body/gesture to justify the second appearance, and check hands, shoulders, cutout edges and lighting. Make sure faces and copy do not compete; matching palette alone is not approval.
6. Display: inspect the desktop preview at 792 display pixels and native mobile review at 390, plus portrait circles at about 200 and 48 pixels. `review-mobile.png` is native 390; the labeled `preview-mobile.png` is 780 at 2× and must display at 390. The original reference determines banner type hierarchy, including its intentionally small secondary labels. Do not claim universal mobile fine-print readability. The main offer, source-backed context and necessary metric scope must remain available across the assembled profile, especially its normal-size headline. Record any unexpected text loss, corrupted glyphs, collisions, or illegibility beyond the original's treatment. Do not change layout/font size merely to make a fine label larger.

Run the independent simulated buyer review after technical checks. `create` does this automatically. After offline rendering or changes, use `python -m linkedin_pack review --output PATH --audience 'BUYER PERSONA'`. Give this reviewer only the neutral finished `review-desktop.png`, `review-mobile.png` and a concise prospective-buyer persona. Do not pass the generation brief, evidence ledger, style/reference name, creator explanation, earlier scores, known complaint or expected verdict. It assesses first impression, perceived offer, specificity, memorable visible details, credibility and visual concerns from the assembled profile. It must perform the name/face substitution test and explain the work without assuming brand familiarity. Do not substitute separate-asset review or the creator's defense for this step. This is an AI simulation, not a real human's testimony.

Record findings and changes, not just a numerical score. `qa.json` may say `ready` only when technical checks pass and a current `blind-review.json` has `verdict: accept`, `interchangeability: specific`, visible `distinctive_details`, `brand_independent_clarity: clear`, a nonblank `plain_language_takeaway`, and no material visual or credibility concerns. `memorability` records what professional fact remains with the buyer. Approval is bound to current output hashes, persona and prompt; changes invalidate it. A stale positive review, failed request, old review schema, interchangeable or brand-dependent verdict, blank takeaway, empty distinctive-details list or `accept` with concerns cannot approve current output. Offline `render` never grants visual approval. A revision verdict remains visible and must not automatically trigger another paid image request.

Distinguish technical checks, independent simulated-buyer review, and source/likeness inspection. Do not describe AI review as human feedback. Trace factual claims to the saved evidence and compare the actual source and output portraits visually; record what was inspected and any unresolved uncertainty. If likeness, unsupported proof, specificity, clarity or reference-level polish fails, the package is not finished regardless of earlier scores. Revise the smallest relevant part, rerender and review the changed result. Do not rerun paid image generation when an HTML or copy change resolves the issue. Earlier approvals of duplicated-photo or interchangeable variants are superseded; revised examples need their own current review.
